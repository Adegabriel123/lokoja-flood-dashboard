Methodology

How the dashboard is built

Open, defensible and reproducible. Source shapefiles are retained for traceability; processed WGS 84 GeoJSON files are used for browser visualization.

Data preparation
  • Source data were provided as shapefile datasets (geometry, attributes, projection and metadata retained across .shp, .shx, .dbf, .prj, .sbn, .sbx, .cpg and .shp.xml components).
  • The datasets were checked for their coordinate-reference information. Original layers used WGS 84 / UTM Zone 32N.
  • Relevant layers were transformed to WGS 84 (longitude, latitude) for web display in Leaflet.
  • Invalid polygon geometries (self-intersections, unclosed rings) were repaired before area calculation.
  • Web-ready datasets were exported as GeoJSON under data/.
  • Area calculations were conducted in UTM Zone 32N and stored as summary attributes; the browser does not recompute area from longitude/latitude.
  • Original source files were retained under source-data/ for traceability.
Flood comparison

The dashboard compares three dated spatial observations and does not continuously monitor flood boundaries. Each polygon is presented as a historical snapshot:

  • April 21 — Baseline water extent. May contain permanent river water; do not treat the entire polygon as newly flooded land.
  • July 15 — Principal flooding observation. Mapped water / flood extent.
  • November 2 — Post-flood observation. Water may remain elevated; permanent river water remains present year-round.

Where future work requires newly flooded land, the defensible operation is potential inundation = (July mapped water) − (April baseline water), still called potential because classification errors and imperfect alignment may affect the result.

Exposure screening

Building exposure uses the centroid-in-polygon method:

Ei,t = 1 if the centroid of building i lies inside the flood polygon at time t, else 0.

Nt = Σ Ei,t

The method is fast, simple to explain, and appropriate for preliminary screening. It may miss buildings partially intersected by water when the centroid remains outside, and may include structures whose centroid falls within permanent water. The displayed label is always "Potentially exposed buildings" — never "destroyed", "damaged" or "victims". No damage survey or field verification is identified in the repository.

Weather integration

Current temperature, precipitation, humidity, weather code, and a five-day precipitation forecast are retrieved from the Open-Meteo API for Lokoja (latitude 7.80, longitude 6.73, timezone Africa/Lagos). The request fires on page load and every 30 minutes. A manual refresh button and a "last updated" timestamp are exposed.

Weather is retrieved independently and is never used to redraw the historical flood polygons. If the weather service cannot be reached, the historical GIS layers continue working and the weather cards display "Temporarily unavailable".

Data limitations
  • Unknown classification accuracy unless documented by the lecturer.
  • Unknown ground-validation status.
  • Potential differences in satellite acquisition conditions across the three dates.
  • Possibility of permanent water being included in the baseline polygon.
  • Building-footprint completeness depends on the supplied dataset.
  • Positional uncertainty between building and flood layers.
  • Weather-model uncertainty — Open-Meteo provides model output, not station observation.
  • Absence of verified river-gauge information.
  • Absence of an official emergency-warning feed.
Coordinate system & area calculation

All web-facing GeoJSON layers use WGS 84 geographic coordinates (longitude first, then latitude). Coordinates around Lokoja fall in the expected Nigerian geographic range (≈ 6.72° – 6.81° E, ≈ 7.75° – 7.84° N).

Projected UTM coordinates must not be passed directly into Leaflet as latitude and longitude. Area is therefore not recomputed in the browser; values are read from summary.json, where they were calculated in UTM Zone 32N.

Percentage change between two non-zero observations uses C = (A₂ − A₁) / A₁ × 100. Where the earlier area is zero or missing, the dashboard shows "Not applicable" rather than dividing by zero.

Performance strategy

The complete building dataset is the largest performance risk. The dashboard therefore:

  • Keeps the original building.* shapefile components in source-data/buildings/.
  • Uses the precomputed exposed-buildings.geojson for the default map.
  • Loads the exposed-building layer only after the main flood map is ready.
  • Allows the user to toggle the building layer off.
  • Stores statistical results in summary.json and reads them at runtime instead of recomputing thousands of spatial intersections in the browser.
  • Uses preferCanvas: true for fast point rendering of the 2,293 building centroids.
